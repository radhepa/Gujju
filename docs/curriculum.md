# The curriculum

How the course is put together, and why. This page started as a review of the first 18 units; the changes it led to are described below.

## What the review found

The base was strong: script first, FSRS for every item, wrong answers chosen from lookalikes, transliteration that fades out, sentence practice, graded stories, a grammar guide and 100 automated accuracy checks. The gaps were in the order things were taught and in what was missing.

1. **No real word until unit 3, lesson 5.** Units 1 to 3 went through all 47 letters in dictionary order, and the vowel signs only arrived in unit 4. The first words were picked to fit the letters (ગગન, રથ, મૌન: sky, chariot, silence), not because anyone says them. For heritage learners, who know hundreds of words by ear, being able to read a familiar word early is what keeps them going.
2. **Rare letters early, common ones late.** ઙ and ઞ came in lesson 1 of the consonants; છ (in every છે) came after them.
3. **A1 basics with no lesson.** Saying "not" (નથી) only turned up inside unit 16 and 18 sentences. Plurals, "I have two brothers" (મારે બે ભાઈ છે), where you're from, where you live, your job, your age, the days of the week and "sometimes / always / never" weren't taught at all.
4. **Family stopped at the basics.** No કાકી, મામી, માસા, ફુઆ, ભાભી, સાસુ, પૌત્રી, and nothing on Gujarati calling cousins brother and sister. Family is the main reason heritage learners come.
5. **Numbers stopped at 25.** Gujarati 21 to 99 are irregular, so a learner couldn't follow most prices, ages or times. No દોઢ or અઢી, which come up constantly.
6. **Nothing past A2 in the course.** The ongoing tense, "has gone", "used to", helper verbs (થઈ ગયું, ખાઈ લો), "because" and "if" were all in the grammar guide, with no lessons to practise them.
7. **No stated outcomes.** Units had a grammar tip but didn't say what you'd be able to do afterwards.

## What changed

### The script: five units in order of use

| Unit | Letters | Signs | Words you can read by the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 First letters | અ આ ક મ ન ર લ સ | ા | મા, નામ, નાક, કાન, રસ, સરસ, મસાલા |
| 2 Signs for i, ee, e | ઈ ઇ એ ત દ પ વ ચ જ | ી િ ે | નદી, રાત, પાલક, કેરી, કેસર, સેવ, ચાવી, દિવસ |
| 3 Signs for u, oo, o | ઉ ઊ ઓ ગ બ ખ ઘ ધ થ ભ ફ | ુ ૂ ો | ગુજરાત, ઘી, ઘર, દૂધ, મેથી, લોકો, ભાખરી, ફોન |
| 4 Curled-back letters | ટ ડ ઠ ઢ ણ ળ અં ઐ ઔ | ં ૈ ૌ | પાપડ, લાડુ, બટાકા, મીઠાઈ, ખમણ, ઢોકળાં, થેપલાં, પૈસા |
| 5 The last letters | ય હ શ ષ છ ઝ ઋ અઃ ઙ ઞ | ૃ ઃ | ભજિયાં, ગાંઠિયા, હળદર, ભાષા, છાશ, છોકરો, ઋતુ, દુઃખ |

Every lesson ends in real words, from lesson 1. Most of them are food, so "read a menu" is a literal goal by unit 4. Lookalikes are kept together where they teach something (ઘ and ધ in the same unit, with a lookalikes review), and the rare letters wait until the end. A test (`script lessons only use letters and signs already taught`) makes sure no word uses a letter before it's been introduced.

Unit 6 (joined letters) gained a lesson on doubled letters, the shape learners meet first in મમ્મી and પપ્પા.

### Thirteen new units

**A1: Everyday Gujarati**

- **Not and no**: નથી, the -તો form with નથી (હું માંસ નથી ખાતો), yes-or-no questions with શું, હજી નહીં, કંઈ નહીં.
- **About me**: where you're from (હું સુરતની છું), where you live, what you do, how old you are, "nice to meet you".
- **One and many**: plurals by ending, no plural after numbers, મારે બે ભાઈ છે for family, બધા and ઘણા.
- **Days and weeks**: the days, dates, રોજ, ક્યારેક, હંમેશાં, ક્યારેય.

**A2: Out and about**

- **The whole family**: aunts and uncles by marriage, in-laws, nephews and grandchildren, cousins as brothers, લગ્ન as a plural, "X's phone came".
- **Bigger numbers**: 21 to 29, the ઓગણ- pattern, the fives, દોઢ, અઢી, સવા, પોણા, હજાર, લાખ.
- **In the kitchen**: ingredients, the spice box and વઘાર, cooking verbs, -ઈને for steps, "Ba, how do you make this?"

**Towards B1: Real conversations**

- **Right now, and already**: ખાઈ રહ્યો છું, the perfect (ગયા છે, જોઈ છે), "have you ever", તમે જમ્યા?, present + થી for "have lived for years".
- **When I was little**: the habitual past, જ્યારે … ત્યારે, નહોતા, "was doing", childhood and the old days.
- **Helper verbs**: જવું, લેવું and દેવું as helpers, જતું રહેવું, બંધ / ચાલુ કરી દો.
- **Because and if**: કારણ કે, એટલે, પણ, જો … તો, આપણે vs અમે, મને લાગે છે કે, reported speech.
- **On the phone with home**: કોણ બોલો છો?, સંભળાય છે?, તબિયત કેવી છે?, news, ધ્યાન રાખજો, ચાલો, મૂકું?
- **Festivals and visits**: બેસતું વર્ષ and સાલ મુબારક, Uttarayan and Navratri, પગે લાગવું and blessings, gifts and મોં મીઠું કરો.

### The path

The 32 units are grouped into four stretches with a heading each: Read Gujarati, Everyday Gujarati (A1), Out and about (A2) and Real conversations (B1). Every unit now starts with what you'll be able to do by the end. The level estimate counts the new units, and B1 now needs the last six.

## Keeping progress

Unit and lesson ids are permanent progress keys, so new units got new ids (u19 to u31) and the path order comes from each unit's `order`. Words that moved between units kept their ids, so their review schedules carry on. The script got new lesson ids (s01 to s05) because the old lessons don't map onto the new ones; anyone who finished the old script gets the new one marked done (`completedLessons` in `src/lib/progress.ts`). Someone halfway through the old script starts the new one, keeps every letter's review schedule, and can test out of any unit they already know.
